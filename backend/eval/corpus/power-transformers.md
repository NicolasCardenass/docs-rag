# Power transformers

A transformer transfers electrical energy between two circuits through electromagnetic induction.
It has a primary and a secondary winding around a shared magnetic core.

The voltage ratio between the windings equals their turns ratio. A step-up transformer increases
voltage for long-distance transmission, which reduces current and therefore resistive losses in
the lines. A step-down transformer lowers the voltage near consumers.

Transformer losses are divided into core losses (hysteresis and eddy currents), which exist
whenever the transformer is energized, and copper losses, which grow with the square of the load
current.
